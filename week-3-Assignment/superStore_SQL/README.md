# Week 3 Data Engineering Assignment — Superstore SQL Analytics

## Summary
In this assignment, I worked with a relational database containing transaction records for a retail Superstore. The goal was to write advanced SQL queries to perform business intelligence analysis. The database includes three main tables: customers, products, and orders, which allow us to track sales trends, customer behavior, and product performance.

## Database Setup
I created a database called `superstore_data` in MySQL. I defined the table structures and constraints. Due to the structure of the raw Superstore dataset, `product_id` was kept without a primary key in the products table because duplicate product IDs map to different names. I loaded the raw CSV data into a temporary `superstore_raw` table using the MySQL Workbench Table Data Import Wizard, populated the normalized tables using INSERT statements, and performed data quality checks to validate data integrity (identifying duplicates, null values, and validating order vs. shipping dates).

## Section A — Subqueries (Q1–Q5)

**Q1.** I wrote a subquery to select all orders where sales are greater than the overall average sales across all orders. This helps identify high-value, above-average orders.

**Q2.** I used a correlated subquery to find the highest-value order (maximum sales) placed by each individual customer.

**Q3.** I wrote a nested subquery to identify customers whose total sales are greater than the average total sales of all customers, helping pinpoint the top-spending buyers.

**Q4.** I used a subquery to retrieve products whose total quantity sold is higher than the average quantity sold across all products, identifying the most popular items.

**Q5.** I used a NOT EXISTS subquery to find products from the products table that have never been ordered, showing items with zero sales.

## Section B — Common Table Expressions (Q1–Q7)

**Q1.** I defined a CTE to aggregate total sales per customer and sorted the results to show the top spenders.

**Q2.** I wrote a CTE to calculate total sales grouped by geographic region to identify the top-performing markets.

**Q3.** I used a CTE to compute the total profit generated by each customer, helping highlight our most profitable relationships.

**Q4.** I wrote a CTE to extract the year and month from order dates, aggregating total monthly sales to observe seasonal trends.

**Q5.** I created a CTE for customer sales and filtered it to list only those customers whose total sales exceeded the average customer revenue.

**Q6.** I used a CTE to count the number of unique orders placed by each customer to measure purchasing frequency.

**Q7.** I wrote a CTE to compute the average order value (AOV) for each customer.

## Section C — Window Functions (Q1–Q5)

**Q1.** I applied the `ROW_NUMBER()` function to rank all customers globally based on their total sales.

**Q2.** I used the `RANK()` function partitioned by region to rank customers within their respective geographic regions.

**Q3.** I used `DENSE_RANK()` partitioned by category to rank products based on total sales within each product category.

**Q4.** I calculated a cumulative running total of daily sales using the SUM window function ordered by order date.

**Q5.** I used the `LAG()` window function partitioned by customer and ordered by order date to calculate the difference in sales between a customer's current order and their previous order.

## Section D — Joins, CTEs, and Window Functions (Q1–Q3)

**Q1.** I combined JOINs, CTEs, and `DENSE_RANK()` to rank all customers by their total sales.

**Q2.** I wrote a query with multiple rankings using CTEs to rank each customer's total sales and total profit side-by-side using the `RANK()` function.

**Q3.** I combined CTEs and JOINs to identify and rank the products by sales within each product category using `DENSE_RANK()`.

## Section E — Business Queries (Q1–Q6)

**Q1.** I wrote a query to rank states by their total profit using a CTE and the `DENSE_RANK()` function.

**Q2.** I joined the customers and orders tables to retrieve the first (minimum) and latest (maximum) order dates for each customer.

**Q3.** I wrote a query to identify products that are currently operating at a net loss (total profit less than zero).

**Q4.** I calculated total daily sales to help identify spikes and trends in daily revenue.

**Q5.** I calculated the total profit generated by each product category to evaluate general category performance.

**Q6.** I aggregated the total number of orders placed by each customer to see our most active buyers.
